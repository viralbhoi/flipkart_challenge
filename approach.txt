FLIPKART GRID HACKATHON – COMPLETE APPROACH & DECISION FLOW

=========================================================
1. PROBLEM UNDERSTANDING
=========================================================

Goal:
Predict traffic demand using geographical, temporal,
road, weather and infrastructure information.

Available Features:
- Geohash
- Day
- Timestamp
- RoadType
- NumberOfLanes
- LargeVehicles
- Landmarks
- Weather
- Temperature

Target:
- Demand

Evaluation:
Score = max(0, 100 × R²)

Initial Hypothesis:

Demand may depend on:

- Location
- Time
- Road Infrastructure
- Weather Conditions

Since no domain knowledge was provided, we decided to
let the data reveal the most important drivers.


=========================================================
2. DATASET EXPLORATION
=========================================================

What We Did:

- Loaded train and test datasets
- Checked schema
- Checked missing values
- Checked cardinality of features
- Studied target distribution

What We Found:

Train Rows ≈ 77K
Test Rows ≈ 41K

The dataset contains both spatial and temporal
information, indicating a spatio-temporal forecasting
problem.

We also observed:

- Very few meaningful missing values
- Geohash had high cardinality
- Target distribution appeared stable

Decision:

Proceed with detailed analysis of:

1. Time
2. Geography
3. Road Features
4. Weather Features


=========================================================
3. TARGET ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

- Demand histogram
- Demand boxplots
- Descriptive statistics
- Day-wise demand analysis

What We Found:

- Demand distribution was relatively stable
- No extreme anomalies
- No obvious requirement for log transformation

Decision:

Keep target unchanged and focus on feature discovery.


=========================================================
4. TIME ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Extracted:

- Hour
- Minute
- Minute of Day

Analyzed:

- Average demand by hour
- Average demand by timestamp

What We Found:

Demand changes throughout the day.

Certain periods consistently showed:

- Higher traffic
- Lower traffic

However:

Demand variation caused purely by time was moderate.

Quantitative Finding:

Hour Signal ≈ 2.8%

Interpretation:

Time contributes useful information,
but cannot explain demand by itself.

Decision:

Keep time features.

Create:

- hour
- minute
- minute_of_day

Later add cyclic encoding:

- hour_sin
- hour_cos


=========================================================
5. GEOGRAPHICAL ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Created hierarchical geographical features:

Geohash
↓
Geo4
Geo5
Geo6

Analyzed:

- Mean demand by region
- Region frequency
- Region distribution

What We Found:

Huge demand differences across locations.

Some regions consistently showed much higher demand.

Demand differences between locations were far larger
than demand differences between hours.

Quantitative Finding:

Geo5 Signal ≈ 15.7%

Interpretation:

Location is one of the strongest drivers of demand.

Decision:

Create hierarchical location features:

- geo4
- geo5
- geo6

Also create:

- geo4_freq
- geo5_freq
- geo6_freq


=========================================================
6. GEOHASH DECODING
=========================================================

What We Did:

Converted:

Geohash
↓
Latitude
Longitude

Generated:

- lat
- lon

Reason:

Geohash is categorical.

Latitude and longitude provide continuous spatial
information.

What We Found:

Locations formed meaningful geographical clusters.

Decision:

Keep both:

- Raw geohash hierarchy
- Continuous coordinates

Final Features:

- geo4
- geo5
- geo6
- lat
- lon


=========================================================
7. ROAD FEATURE ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Analyzed:

RoadType vs Demand

NumberOfLanes vs Demand

LargeVehicles vs Demand

What We Found:

RoadType showed extremely strong separation.

Example:

Highway Demand
>
Street Demand
>
Residential Demand

RoadType became one of the strongest predictors.

NumberOfLanes and LargeVehicles also showed strong
relationships.

Decision:

Keep all road-related features.

Features retained:

- RoadType
- NumberOfLanes
- LargeVehicles


=========================================================
8. WEATHER ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Analyzed:

- Weather vs Demand
- Temperature vs Demand
- Weather by Hour
- Temperature Trends

What We Found:

Almost no variation.

Demand remained nearly identical across:

- Sunny
- Rainy
- Foggy
- Snowy

Temperature also showed negligible impact.

Mutual Information scores were extremely low.

Interpretation:

Weather contains very weak signal.

Decision:

Keep weather features because CatBoost can process them
cheaply, but avoid spending feature engineering effort
on weather interactions.


=========================================================
9. SIGNAL DISCOVERY PHASE
=========================================================

At this point we stopped traditional EDA.

Instead we asked:

"What actually explains demand?"

=========================================================
9.1 FEATURE IMPORTANCE ANALYSIS
=========================================================

Methods Used:

- Mutual Information
- Group Statistics
- Variance Analysis

What We Found:

Strong Features:

- RoadType
- Geo6
- Geohash
- NumberOfLanes
- LargeVehicles

Weak Features:

- Weather
- Temperature

Decision:

Focus engineering efforts on:

Location
+
Road Infrastructure
+
Time


=========================================================
9.2 GEO STABILITY ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Compared regional demand across different days.

What We Found:

Most regions maintained similar demand behavior.

Interpretation:

Historical regional demand is meaningful.

Decision:

Create target aggregation features using regional
historical demand.


=========================================================
9.3 GEO COVERAGE ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Compared:

Train Geo Values
vs
Test Geo Values

What We Found:

Almost every Geo5 region present in test data already
existed in training data.

Interpretation:

Historical regional statistics should generalize well.

Decision:

Proceed with target encoding features.


=========================================================
9.4 GEO-TIME INTERACTION ANALYSIS
=========================================================

What We Did:

Created:

Geo5 × Hour heatmaps

Studied:

Demand behavior across both location and time.

What We Found:

Different regions have completely different hourly
patterns.

Example:

Commercial Areas:
- Morning peaks

Residential Areas:
- Evening peaks

Quantitative Finding:

Geo5 Signal      ≈ 15.7%
Hour Signal      ≈ 2.8%
Geo5+Hour Signal ≈ 22.4%

Most Important Discovery:

Location-Time interactions contain more information
than location or time individually.

Decision:

Build location-time aggregation features.


=========================================================
10. FEATURE ENGINEERING
=========================================================

Based on every previous finding.

---------------------------------------------------------
10.1 Time Features
---------------------------------------------------------

Created:

- hour
- minute
- minute_of_day

Added:

- hour_sin
- hour_cos

Reason:

Capture cyclic nature of time.


---------------------------------------------------------
10.2 Spatial Features
---------------------------------------------------------

Created:

- geo4
- geo5
- geo6

Reason:

Capture demand at multiple geographical resolutions.


---------------------------------------------------------
10.3 Frequency Features
---------------------------------------------------------

Created:

- geo4_freq
- geo5_freq
- geo6_freq

Reason:

Dense regions behave differently from sparse regions.


---------------------------------------------------------
10.4 Coordinate Features
---------------------------------------------------------

Created:

- lat
- lon

Reason:

Capture continuous spatial relationships.


---------------------------------------------------------
10.5 Historical Demand Features
---------------------------------------------------------

Created:

- geo4_mean_demand
- geo5_mean_demand
- geo6_mean_demand

Reason:

Geo stability analysis showed historical demand is
predictive.

Finding That Led To This:

Regions consistently exhibited similar demand patterns.


---------------------------------------------------------
10.6 Geo-Time Demand Features
---------------------------------------------------------

Created:

- geo4_hour_mean
- geo5_hour_mean
- geo6_hour_mean

Reason:

Geo-Time analysis revealed that demand is fundamentally
a location-time interaction problem.

Finding That Led To This:

Geo5+Hour signal was substantially larger than
Geo5 signal alone.

These ultimately became the strongest engineered
features.


=========================================================
11. MODEL SELECTION
=========================================================

Models Evaluated:

- Ridge
- HistGradientBoosting
- LightGBM
- XGBoost
- CatBoost

What We Found:

CatBoost consistently performed best.

Reason:

- Handles categorical features natively
- Handles high-cardinality geospatial features
- Learns complex interactions effectively
- Requires minimal preprocessing


=========================================================
12. FINAL INSIGHT
=========================================================

Every stage of analysis progressively reduced the search
space.

Initial Assumption:

Demand depends on:
- Location
- Time
- Weather
- Road Conditions

EDA Findings:

Weather signal was weak.

RoadType signal was strong.

Geographical signal was very strong.

Signal Discovery Findings:

Location-Time interactions explained the largest
portion of variance.

Feature Engineering Decision:

Focus on:

- Spatial Features
- Spatial Aggregations
- Spatial-Temporal Aggregations

Final Understanding:

Traffic Demand ≈

(Location)
+
(Road Infrastructure)
+
(Location × Time Interaction)

This insight became the foundation of the final model.
